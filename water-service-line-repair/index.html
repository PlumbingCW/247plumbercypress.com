Emergency Water Service Line Repair in Cypress, CA

The service line carries water from the meter at the street to your house. When it breaks, you may see a soggy lawn, a sinkhole in the planter or a sudden drop in pressure. Close the house side valve and call our 24/7 line.

Where the service line starts and stops

The line runs from the water provider's meter, through the yard or driveway, to the main shutoff at the house. In Cypress the water provider is Golden State Water Company, which owns the meter and the connection to its main. The portion on the property side of the meter is generally the owner's responsibility, though you should confirm the boundary with the utility before work.

If the leak is on the utility side of the meter, we help you report it. If it is on your side, we locate and repair it.

Signs and first steps

Wet ground

A soggy patch or an unexplained green strip in dry weather often marks the line.

Meter movement

Turn off every fixture and watch the meter. Movement confirms a leak.

Pressure drop

A sudden fall in pressure at every faucet points to a break upstream.

Shut it off

Close the valve at the house if you can reach it and tell us where it is.

Repair, replace or reroute

A short leak on a sound line can be fixed with a coupling. A line with several leaks, or one made of old galvanized steel, is better replaced. Trenchless methods can pull a new line through the old path with small entry pits, which saves landscaping and driveways.

We explain the choices after locating the leak. Locating methods are covered on the leak detection page.

Pressure and hard water matter here

High street pressure strains older service lines, and a pressure regulator near the house protects the plumbing inside. Golden State Water reports an average hardness of 237 mg/L, or about 13.8 grains per gallon, for its West Orange County system.

Readings in its 2025 data ranged from roughly 63 to 369 mg/L. Scale narrows pipes over decades and reduces flow, which is another reason old lines are replaced and not patched.

Pressure regulators and shutoff valves at the house

Where the service line enters the house, a main shutoff valve and often a pressure regulator protect the plumbing. A regulator that has failed lets street pressure through, which strains every fixture. A shutoff valve that is seized will not close in an emergency.

During a service line repair we test both and replace them if they are worn. It is easier to do it while the line is open than to come back later.

Questions to ask before a service line replacement

  • Where is the boundary between the utility line and the line on my property?
  • What material is the existing line?
  • Will the work cross a driveway, sidewalk or tree root zone?
  • Does the work need a permit or an encroachment approval?
  • How will the water shutoff be scheduled?

Standards and checking a plumber's license

Plumbing work for water service line repair in California is governed by the California Plumbing Code, and plumbing contractors must hold a license from the Contractors State License Board.

You can look up any contractor on the CSLB License Check tool at cslb.ca.gov, or call 1-800-321-CSLB (2752). Ask for our license details when you call and check them yourself before work begins.
