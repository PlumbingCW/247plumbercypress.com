Water Softener and Filtration in Cypress, CA

Hard water leaves white scale on faucets, spots on glass and a film in showers. It also shortens the life of water heaters and appliances. A softener or filter changes that at the source.

Water softener and filtration service in Cypress, CA includes hardness testing, installation, repair and replacement of whole house systems.

What the water in Cypress looks like

Golden State Water reports an average hardness of 237 mg/L, or about 13.8 grains per gallon, for its West Orange County system. Readings in its 2025 data ranged from roughly 63 to 369 mg/L.

On the common USGS scale, anything above 180 mg/L counts as very hard, so the average sits in that top band. The supply is a blend of local groundwater and imported water, and the mix can shift through the year.

Hard water is safe to drink. The cost is in appliances, fixtures and the time you spend scrubbing. If hardness is your complaint, a softener addresses it, and a filter addresses taste, odor or sediment.

What a softener does for your plumbing

Scale builds up inside water heaters, on heating elements and in showerheads. Softened water slows that buildup, so tank heaters last longer and tankless units need less flushing. See water heater repair and tankless water heater for how scale shows up.

Softened water can also reduce soap use and leave fixtures cleaner. It is worth asking whether you want one tap left unsoftened, because some people prefer the taste of unsoftened water or have health advice to limit sodium.

What to expect from the first month

After installation, the softener fills its tank with salt and begins its regeneration cycles, usually at night. In the first weeks, you may notice that the water feels different on the skin and that soap lathers more easily. Scale on existing fixtures does not vanish overnight, but new buildup stops forming.

We show you how to check the salt level, how to read the control head and how to bypass the system if you ever need to. Most systems need only a refill of salt every month or two.

Questions to ask before buying a softener

  • What is my current hardness, measured on site?
  • How many people live in the home?
  • Where will the unit sit, and where does it drain?
  • Should one tap, such as the kitchen cold tap, stay unsoftened?
  • How much salt does the model use per cycle?

Standards and checking a plumber's license

Plumbing work for water softener and filtration in California is governed by the California Plumbing Code, and plumbing contractors must hold a license from the Contractors State License Board.

You can look up any contractor on the CSLB License Check tool at cslb.ca.gov, or call 1-800-321-CSLB (2752). Ask for our license details when you call and check them yourself before work begins.
