Residential Sewer Line Cleaning: A Cypress, CA Homeowner Guide

By the Cypress 24/7 Plumbing team. Published November 12, 2026.

A residential sewer line needs cleaning when drains slow in more than one fixture, floor drains gurgle or sewage backs up into a tub. The cleaning itself uses a heavy cable or a high pressure water jet to clear roots, grease and debris, followed by a camera check.

This guide covers the warning signs, the methods, how often to clean and how the regional sewer work in Cypress relates to the pipe under your own yard.

Warning signs

Look for several fixtures draining slowly, a toilet that gurgles when the washing machine drains, sewage smells outside near the cleanout and wet spots in the yard along the line. Any of these suggests the main line is partly blocked.

A full backup is an emergency. Stop using water and call. The 24/7 sewer line cleaning service can clear it.

How cleaning is done

A technician opens the cleanout and feeds in a cable machine with a cutting head or a jetting hose. The cable cuts roots and breaks through blockages. The jetter scours the pipe wall. Then a camera travels the line and records the result.

A line that is open but not inspected can fail again soon. The camera is how you know whether the cause was a temporary blockage or a pipe defect.

The Cypress Trunk Sewer and your lateral

The Orange County Sanitation District has been rehabilitating the Cypress Trunk Sewer, a regional line about fifty years old and roughly seven miles long that serves Cypress, Los Alamitos and La Palma.

The plan is to replace about a mile with larger pipe, chemically grout and clean around five and a half miles and rehabilitate or replace about a hundred manholes. Construction was expected to begin in summer 2025 and run about three years.

That project improves the shared line in the street. The lateral between your house and the street is a separate pipe. A homeowner in La Palma or Los Alamitos still benefits from a cleaning and a camera look every few years, especially if mature trees are nearby.

How often to clean

There is no single schedule. A newer PVC line with no trees nearby may never need cleaning. An older clay or cast iron lateral with roots may need it yearly. The camera footage from the first cleaning sets the interval.

Keep grease, wipes and paper towels out of the line, and keep a clear path to the cleanout. See hydro jetting for repeated grease problems.

What to do while you wait for the technician

  • Stop running water in the house.
  • Keep people and pets away from any surfaced sewage.
  • Do not use chemical cleaners.
  • Clear access to the cleanout.
  • Photograph any damage for your insurer.

Costs and what shapes them

Cleaning costs depend on the equipment used, the length of the line, how easy the cleanout is to reach and how severe the blockage is. A camera adds time but gives you a record. We explain the price before work starts, and the explanation includes why one method was chosen over another.

Homeowners often ask whether insurance pays. Cleaning itself is a maintenance cost, but damage from a backup may be covered under an endorsement. Ask your carrier before an emergency and not during one.

What roots do to older pipes

Roots seek water and nutrients, and a joint that weeps slightly is an invitation. Hair thin roots enter, grow into a mat and catch paper. The mat narrows the pipe until it stops. Cutting the roots clears the line, but they regrow unless the joint is sealed.

That cycle explains why some homeowners schedule cleaning every year. A repair at the joint, such as a section replacement or a liner, ends the cycle and is worth discussing after the camera shows where the roots enter.

Questions to ask the technician

Ask what tool they will use and why. Ask whether they will run a camera before and after. Ask where the cleanout is and who will open it. Ask what they found and request the video. A good technician answers all of these directly.

Protecting the line between visits

Choose a schedule you can keep. Pour cooking grease into a can and put it in the trash. Use a drain screen in the kitchen sink. Avoid planting thirsty trees near the lateral. If the line has a history, note the cleaning date on your calendar and have the camera footage handy for the next technician.

When cleaning is not enough

If the camera shows a broken, offset or sagging pipe, cleaning only buys time. That is the point to discuss sewer line repair options with the footage in front of you.
