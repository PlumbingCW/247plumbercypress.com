Emergency Hydro Jetting in Cypress, CA

Hydro jetting uses a high pressure water stream to scour the inside of a drain or sewer line. It is the answer when a cable keeps opening a hole in the clog and the line fills again a few weeks later.

Hydro jetting in Cypress, CA is available at any hour for backups, and as a planned cleaning for restaurants, apartments and homes with a repeat clog history.

What hydro jetting actually does

A jetting hose carries a nozzle with rear facing jets. Water at high pressure drives the nozzle forward and scrubs the pipe wall as it returns, cutting grease, scale, silt and small roots away and flushing them downstream. A snake can leave a ring of residue that rebuilds the clog. A jetter removes the residue.

It does not repair a broken pipe. That is why we run a camera first, and why we may recommend a sewer line repair if the jetting shows a collapsed or offset section.

Where jetting makes the biggest difference

Kitchen drain lines

Cooking grease cools and coats the pipe wall in layers. Jetting strips it back to bare pipe.

Root intrusion

Fine roots entering at joints are cut and flushed. Heavier roots may need cutting heads first.

Restaurants and shops

Grease heavy lines can be scheduled for regular jetting, see commercial plumbing.

Main sewer lines

Cleaning the full diameter of the line restores flow and lowers the odds of another backup.

Is it safe for older Cypress pipes?

Jetting pressure is set to match the pipe. Cast iron and clay lines are common in Cypress homes built before the 1970s, and a technician reads the camera view before choosing a pressure and a nozzle. Pipes that are cracked or badly offset are not jetted aggressively, because water can wash soil into the break.

If the camera shows damage, we tell you and show you the footage. You decide the next step.

Planned jetting for businesses and apartments

Restaurants along Katella Avenue and Valley View Street and apartment buildings with shared lines see grease and wipe buildup faster than single homes. A jetting schedule set by what the camera shows costs far less than a weekend backup that closes a kitchen.

We recommend the interval after seeing your line, not before. A line that is clean after one visit may need nothing for a year, and a grease heavy line may need quarterly attention.

Questions to ask before a jetting visit

  • Where is the cleanout, and is it accessible?
  • How often has the line backed up in the past year?
  • Is the pipe cast iron, clay, ABS or PVC?
  • Has anyone ever run a camera through it?
  • Are there trees near the line?

What we check before the jetter goes in

Before water goes down the line, we confirm the pipe material, look for a clear path with a camera where possible and locate the cleanout. These steps prevent surprises, such as a collapsed section that the jetter cannot pass or a line that connects somewhere unexpected.

Standards and checking a plumber's license

Plumbing work for hydro jetting in California is governed by the California Plumbing Code, and plumbing contractors must hold a license from the Contractors State License Board.

You can look up any contractor on the CSLB License Check tool at cslb.ca.gov, or call 1-800-321-CSLB (2752). Ask for our license details when you call and check them yourself before work begins.
