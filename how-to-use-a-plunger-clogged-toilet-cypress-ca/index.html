How to Use a Plunger on a Clogged Toilet: What Cypress, CA Homes Need to Know

By the Cypress 24/7 Plumbing team. Published November 12, 2026.

Use a flange plunger, which has a rubber extension below the cup, and seat it fully in the drain opening. Push down gently first to expel air, then pump steadily for fifteen to twenty seconds. Most clogs clear in a few rounds.

This guide covers the right tool, the technique, what to avoid and how to tell when the problem is in the drain line and not the toilet.

Choose the right plunger

A flat cup plunger is made for sinks and tubs. A flange plunger fits the curve of the toilet drain and makes a better seal. Make sure the cup is soft and flexible, not cracked or hardened.

You also need enough water in the bowl to cover the cup. If the bowl is nearly empty, add water from a bucket. If it is overflowing, close the supply valve behind the toilet first.

Plunger steps
StepActionWhy
1.Close the supply valve if water is rising.Stops more water from entering.
2.Seat the flange in the drain.Creates a seal.
3.Push gently once.Expels trapped air.
4.Pump firmly 15 to 20 times.Moves water back and forth.
5.Pull off sharply on the last stroke.Breaks the clog loose.
6.Check the bowl and flush once.Confirms the drain is open.

What not to do

Do not flush repeatedly hoping it will clear. Each flush adds water. Do not pour chemical drain cleaners into a full bowl, because they sit in standing water and splash. Do not push a coat hanger down the drain, which scratches porcelain.

If several attempts fail, stop. Forcing it can crack the bowl or push a clog deeper. A toilet auger, designed for the toilet trap, is the next tool.

Prevention habits

  • Flush only toilet paper and human waste.
  • Keep wipes, cotton products and paper towels out of the toilet.
  • Use less paper in a household with an older, low flow toilet.
  • Teach children not to flush toys.
  • Have a toilet auger on hand.

Using a toilet auger

A toilet auger has a curved head that protects the porcelain. Feed it into the drain, crank the handle and push gently until it catches the clog, then retrieve or break it up. Flush to check.

If the auger meets a hard obstruction, such as a toy, stop. A plumber can retrieve it without damaging the toilet.

Why toilets clog in the first place

Most clogs come from too much paper at once, from wipes that do not break down or from objects that were never meant for the toilet. Low flow fixtures use less water per flush, which helps the environment but leaves less force to move a large load through the trap.

The trap itself is a curved passage built into the toilet. It holds water as a seal against sewer gas and is the narrowest point in the system. Almost every household clog sits there, which is why a plunger works so often.

Wipes labeled flushable

Wipes sold as flushable do not break up the way paper does. They collect in the lateral and wrap around roots and rough joints. If your household uses them, switch to the trash can for a few months and see whether clogs stop.

Teaching the household one routine

Put the plunger, a pair of gloves and a bucket where everyone can find them. Agree that the first response to a rising bowl is to close the supply valve behind the toilet. Children can learn that in a minute, and it stops most overflows.

Keep our number visible too. A clog that comes back every week is a drain line problem, and the sooner it is diagnosed, the less it costs.

One more reason to act early

A partly blocked toilet that is flushed again and again can overflow in the middle of the night. Treat a slow flush as a warning. Plunge once, and if the problem repeats within a week, call a plumber to look at the drain line.

When to call for help

Call if the toilet will not clear after a few careful tries, if it overflows again soon after clearing, if more than one fixture is affected or if there is a sewage smell. See toilet repair for services that cover clogs and weak flushes.
