The quick shutoff that limits the damage

Every sink should have two small valves under it called angle stops, one for hot and one for cold. Turn each clockwise until it stops. If they are corroded and will not move, close the main valve instead and tell the dispatcher so the technician can bring replacement stops.

Water spraying from the handle or the base of the faucet usually means a failed cartridge or seal. Water dripping from the spout points to a worn washer or seat.

Repair or replace a faucet

Repair makes sense when the faucet is a decent brand with parts available. Replacement is a better choice when the finish has corroded, the body is pitted or the cost of parts and labor approaches the price of a new fixture.

Hard water leaves white scale on aerators and inside cartridges. Golden State Water reports an average hardness of 237 mg/L, or about 13.8 grains per gallon, for its West Orange County system. Readings in its 2025 data ranged from roughly 63 to 369 mg/L. Cleaning aerators and installing a softener in the home both reduce the frequency of repairs.

Choosing a replacement faucet

If repair is not worth it, a few choices make the new faucet last. Solid brass bodies outlast plastic ones. A cartridge design with widely available parts is easier to service. A finish rated for hard water resists spotting better than a delicate one.

We install the new faucet, replace worn supply lines and angle stops while the sink is clear, and test every connection. Replacing the stops at the same time is cheap insurance against the next emergency.

Faucet care that prevents repairs

  • Unscrew the aerator every few months and soak it in vinegar to dissolve scale.
  • Do not force a handle that feels stiff. Stiffness is a sign of a worn cartridge.
  • Operate the angle stops once or twice a year so they do not seize.
  • Wipe the base of the faucet and look for slow weeping at the joint.
  • Report a drip early because the seat wears faster as it continues.

Standards and checking a plumber's license

Plumbing work for faucet repair in California is governed by the California Plumbing Code, and plumbing contractors must hold a license from the Contractors State License Board.

You can look up any contractor on the CSLB License Check tool at cslb.ca.gov, or call 1-800-321-CSLB (2752). Ask for our license details when you call and check them yourself before work begins.

Safety checks before you start a faucet repair

  • Close both angle stops, then open the faucet to confirm the water is off.
  • Plug the sink drain so small parts cannot fall in.
  • Take a photo of the assembly before taking it apart.
  • Do not force a seized stop or handle, because it can crack.
  • Turn the water back on slowly and check every joint.

When the problem is bigger than the faucet

Low pressure across the house is not a faucet problem. A pressure regulator, a partly closed valve or a corroded supply line is the likelier cause, and our technicians check that during the visit. For water that is running with nothing open, see leak detection.
