Underground Plumbing Leak Detection: A Cypress, CA Homeowner Guide

By the Cypress 24/7 Plumbing team. Published November 12, 2026.

An underground plumbing leak shows itself in indirect ways: a water bill that climbs, a meter that moves when everything is off, a soggy patch of lawn on a dry day or a warm spot on the floor. The simplest first check is the water meter.

This guide walks through the signs, a meter test you can do in ten minutes and how professionals locate buried leaks without digging holes.

Signs of a buried leak

Common signs include a sudden rise in the water bill and the sound of running water when nothing is on. Others are low pressure at every tap, a wet or unusually green strip of yard, a puddle near the meter and a floor that feels warm. A single sign may have a simple cause. Several together point to a leak.

Water that comes up in a yard is the easy case. Water under a slab shows up as warm floors, cracked tile or damp baseboards.

How professionals find the leak

The crew first confirms the leak with a pressure test of the isolated system. Then they narrow it with acoustic listening devices that pick up the hiss of escaping water through soil or concrete, and with thermal cameras that show warm lines. Tracer gas is sometimes used for stubborn cases.

Pinpointing matters because the repair is about access. A precise mark means a small opening. See the leak detection page for the full process.

Cypress water and your meter

Cypress homes get their water from Golden State Water Company. The meter is the utility's, and it is the best instrument you have for finding a leak. Read it, shut off everything and read it again after an hour. A change means a leak, and the pattern tells you whether it is steady or intermittent.

Most homes in the area sit on slabs, so hot water lines under the floor are a common culprit. Copper laid in the 1960s and 1970s is at an age when pinholes appear, and the area's hard water adds stress. If the slab is involved, slab leak repair covers the repair paths. Neighbors in Stanton and Buena Park run the same meter test.

The ten minute meter test

  • Turn off every faucet, appliance and irrigation zone.
  • Locate the meter box, usually near the curb, and lift the lid carefully.
  • Look at the leak indicator, a small triangle or dial that spins with low flow.
  • Note the reading, wait an hour without using water, and read again.
  • If the reading changed, close the house main and check again to see whether the leak is before or after that valve.

Why waiting makes it worse

A buried leak does not stop and it does not stay small. Water finds a path, saturates soil and can undermine a slab or a driveway. Each week adds to the water bill and to the repair scope. Insurance carriers also look at how long a leak ran, so quick action protects a claim.

If you suspect a leak, do the meter test, note the results and call. The sooner the line is isolated, the smaller the repair.

Do not guess with a shovel

Digging at random in the yard can cut other lines, such as gas, electric or irrigation. Call 811 before digging deep, and let a locator mark the leak first. Precision beats effort every time.

Slab, yard or irrigation: who owns what

A leak under the house is the homeowner's to repair. A leak before the meter belongs to the utility. Irrigation lines are often mistaken for supply leaks, so close the irrigation valve and re-check the meter before assuming the worst.

Pool and spa lines add another possibility. If you have one, include it when you describe the symptoms.

Keeping records for your insurer and your seller

Photograph the meter readings, the damp areas and the repair. Save the invoice and the leak location report. If a leak caused damage, these records support a claim. If you sell the house later, the paperwork shows the leak was found and fixed properly.

What to do next

If the meter test confirms a leak, shut the main valve and call. Photograph any damp areas and keep your water bill. If the leak is under a slab, resist the urge to wait. Persistent leaks erode soil and can soften the base under the floor.

A service line repair covers the pipe from the meter to the house. Inside the house, a burst pipe repair visit handles the supply lines.
