When a water heater failure is an emergency

Water heaters rarely fail politely. A tank that has rusted through at the base releases dozens of gallons within an hour, and a gas unit that will not light leaves a household without hot water in the middle of a work week. Both cases call for a fast decision about repair versus replacement, and the age of the tank usually settles it.

Callers describe the same handful of starting points. Water around the base of the tank, no hot water at all, rusty hot water, rumbling or popping from the tank, and a pilot that keeps going out. Our dispatcher asks where the water is coming from, how old the unit is and whether it is gas or electric, then sends a technician who can quote the swap on site.

Repair or replace: a decision guide

Most tank water heaters are worth replacing once they pass about ten years of service or when the tank itself leaks. A leak from the tank body cannot be patched.

A leak from a fitting, a relief valve or the drain valve often can, which is why the first visit starts with finding the exact source. If the tank is sound and the unit is young, our water heater repair visit costs far less than a new unit.

Cypress water, permits and what that means for your new unit

Cypress homes receive water from Golden State Water Company. Golden State Water reports an average hardness of 237 mg/L, or about 13.8 grains per gallon, for its West Orange County system. Readings in its 2025 data ranged from roughly 63 to 369 mg/L.

Water in that range leaves mineral scale inside a tank, which is why heaters in this area often wear out near the low end of their life expectancy. A water softener upstream of the heater slows that scale, and the water softener page explains how a hardness test works.

The City of Cypress lists water heaters among the fixed appliances that need a building permit, and inspections run Monday through Friday from 8:00 a.m. to 4:00 p.m. with about two days of notice.

We pull the permit for the swap so the inspection fits the install rather than the other way around. The Building Division can be reached at (714) 229-6730 if you want to confirm anything yourself.

What to do in the first ten minutes of a tank failure

  • Turn off the cold water supply valve on the pipe above the heater.
  • Turn the gas control knob to pilot or off, or switch off the dedicated breaker for an electric unit.
  • Open one hot tap upstairs so the tank can drain without a vacuum.
  • Move stored boxes and anything electrical away from the water.
  • Photograph the unit, the label and the water for your insurance file, then call the 24/7 line.

What a Cypress homeowner can expect during the swap

Most replacements take a few hours. The old tank is drained, disconnected and carried out, the new unit is set on a pan and connected to the supply and gas or electric lines, and the relief valve discharge, venting and expansion control are checked before the heater is lit. Older Cypress garages often have the heater in a tight closet, so tell us about access when you call.

Homes built in the 1960s and 1970s, which describes much of the city after the dairy farms sold to developers, sometimes have original shutoff valves that will not close fully. If yours will not, we replace the valve at the same visit so the next failure does not become a flood.

Standards and checking a plumber's license

Plumbing work for water heater replacement in California is governed by the California Plumbing Code, and plumbing contractors must hold a license from the Contractors State License Board.

You can look up any contractor on the CSLB License Check tool at cslb.ca.gov, or call 1-800-321-CSLB (2752). Ask for our license details when you call and check them yourself before work begins.
