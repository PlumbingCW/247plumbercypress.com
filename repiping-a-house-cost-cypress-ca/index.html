Repiping a House Cost: A Cypress, CA Price Guide

By the Cypress 24/7 Plumbing team. Published November 12, 2026.

The cost of repiping a house depends on five things: the size of the home, the pipe material, how easy the walls and attic are to reach, the number of fixtures and whether the house sits on a slab. A one story slab home with open attic access is a very different job from a two story house with finished walls.

This guide explains each cost driver in plain terms so you can compare quotes from any contractor, including us. Because prices change with materials and labor, we do not publish a number here. We give a clear price after a walk through.

The five factors that set the price

The first is the size of the house. More square footage means longer pipe runs and more fittings. The second is the number of fixtures, since each sink, toilet, tub, shower, washer and hose bib needs its own branch. The third is pipe material. PEX is flexible and quick to install, copper costs more in material and labor and CPVC falls in between where allowed.

The fourth is access. Open attics and crawlspaces keep labor low, while finished walls and second floors require more holes and patching. The fifth is whether lines run through the slab. Most Cypress homes are on slabs, and rerouting overhead avoids concrete but adds attic work.

What a quote should include

A good quote lists the pipe material, the number of fixtures, the new shutoff valves, a pressure regulator if needed, the permit and inspection, drywall access and patching, and cleanup. It should say what is not included, such as painting or texture matching, so you can plan for it.

Ask who does the patching. Some contractors patch to a primed surface and leave painting to you. Others bring a drywall crew.

Why repipes are common in Cypress

Cypress was incorporated in 1956 as Dairy City and renamed in 1957, and the dairies were sold to developers in the 1960s. Many homes date from that building boom, and some still have original galvanized or early copper lines. That is why pinhole leaks, rusty water and low pressure are everyday reasons for a repipe here.

The City of Cypress Building Division lists plumbing work among the work that needs a permit, and inspections run Monday through Friday from 8:00 a.m. to 4:00 p.m. with about two days of notice. We handle both. The same age pattern shows up in La Palma and Los Alamitos, so many neighbors weigh the same decision. For the process, see the repiping page.

Ways to keep the cost reasonable

Get the walk through done before a leak turns into water damage, because an emergency adds drywall, flooring and drying costs on top of the plumbing. Combine the repipe with other wall work, such as a bathroom remodel, so the walls are only opened once. Choose a pipe material that fits your goals and not just the lowest bid.

If a single section is the only failure, a repair may be enough, see burst pipe repair. A pattern of pinholes is the trigger for a repipe.

Timing the project well

Plan a repipe outside of busy holiday weeks if you can, since the house will have no water for part of each day. Ask the contractor for a day by day schedule, including when the water will be restored and when inspections are expected.

If you are remodeling a kitchen or bathroom, coordinate so that the plumber works before the finish trades. That sequence saves money and avoids opening a new wall twice. Keep receipts and the permit record, because they matter at resale.

Hidden costs people forget

The plumbing quote may not include repainting patched walls, replacing damaged texture, moving furniture or covering floors. Budget for these, and ask who handles each one. If the house has asbestos containing materials from earlier decades, the contractor should explain how that affects the work.

The permit fee, any required upgrades to shutoff valves or pressure regulators and a post work cleanup should all appear in writing. A written scope protects both sides.

Comparing quotes fairly

Line up each quote by scope before price. Do they use the same pipe? Do they include the permit, the patching and the new shutoff valves? A low number that leaves out half the work is not low. Ask each bidder to walk the house and to put the scope in writing, then compare line by line.

Check that the contractor is licensed for plumbing work with the California Contractors State License Board, using its online license check, and ask for proof of insurance.

Questions to ask every contractor

  • Which pipe material do you recommend and why?
  • How many access holes will be cut, and who patches them?
  • Is the permit included?
  • How will the water be restored each evening?
  • What happens if the walls reveal damage?
