When a hidden leak is an emergency

A hidden leak becomes an emergency the moment water reaches something that can be damaged. A ceiling stain that is growing, a wall that feels warm or cold and damp, water pooling at the edge of the slab or a sound of running water in a silent house all qualify.

If you see active water, close the main valve and call. The leak is easier to find when the water can be isolated and the system pressurized in a controlled way.

The water meter test and your high water bill

The fastest first check is the meter. Turn off every fixture and appliance, then look at the leak indicator on the meter, usually a small triangle or dial. If it moves, water is leaving the system somewhere. If it does not, the leak may be on the home side of a shutoff you can close and test in stages.

Cypress homes are served by Golden State Water Company, which reports its water system data in an annual water quality report. Billing and usage questions go to the utility, and a technician can help you separate a plumbing leak from a change in how the household uses water. A toilet flapper that leaks silently can waste more water than a visible drip.

How we find a leak without tearing things apart

Pressure test

The system is isolated in sections and held at pressure. A falling gauge confirms a leak and shows which section.

Acoustic listening

Sensitive microphones pick up the sound of water escaping from a pressurized line through slab, wall or soil.

Thermal imaging

A thermal camera shows temperature differences from hot water lines and moisture behind drywall. See also slab leak repair.

Camera inspection

A small camera checks drain and sewer lines when the water source is waste water, see sewer inspection.

Ceiling stains, wall damp and crawlspace leaks

A stain on a ceiling is often far from the source. Water travels along framing before it drops, so the damp spot below may be several feet from the pipe or the roof leak above. A bathroom upstairs, a supply line in an attic or a failed water heater drain pan are common culprits.

Homes with raised floors or crawlspaces can hide a leak for months. Musty smells, sagging flooring and a rising water bill are the signs. Cypress houses mostly sit on slabs, but older additions and some homes have crawlspace areas that need a look.

Standards and checking a plumber's license

Plumbing work for leak detection in California is governed by the California Plumbing Code, and plumbing contractors must hold a license from the Contractors State License Board.

You can look up any contractor on the CSLB License Check tool at cslb.ca.gov, or call 1-800-321-CSLB (2752). Ask for our license details when you call and check them yourself before work begins.
