Cypress and the cities around it

Our base is 6550 Katella Avenue in Cypress, ZIP 90630, in Orange County. Cypress is home to about 48,800 residents by our dataset and covers about 6.6 square miles. It borders Los Alamitos, La Palma, Stanton, Buena Park and Cerritos, so a crew leaving our base can reach each neighbor along familiar surface streets.

Golden State Water Company serves Cypress, Los Alamitos and Stanton, and portions of La Palma and other nearby communities, so water quality conditions are similar across much of the area. Details are on the individual city pages.

How dispatch works across the area

Los Alamitos and Rossmoor sit just south and west of Cypress. Homes there date largely from the late 1950s and 1960s, so our crews often find original supply lines and older water heaters.

La Palma is the smallest city in Orange County by area, with about 15,600 residents. It is served by the Cypress Trunk Sewer, which the Orange County Sanitation District is rehabilitating.

Stanton, east of Cypress, has about 38,000 residents and a housing stock split almost evenly between owners and renters. We work with tenants, landlords and managers.

Buena Park is the largest city we serve, with about 84,000 residents, the entertainment district around Knott's Berry Farm and a mix of homes, apartments and restaurants.

When you call, tell the dispatcher your city and cross street. We route by street and not only by ZIP code, since traffic on Katella Avenue, Beach Boulevard and Valley View Street changes the fastest path through the day.

For services, see the full plumbing services list. If your city is not listed, call anyway and ask. We will tell you honestly whether we can reach you.

Water, permits and what changes city to city

Water quality is similar across much of the area because Golden State Water Company serves Cypress, Los Alamitos and Stanton, plus portions of La Palma. Hardness in its West Orange County system averaged about 237 mg/L in 2025 data, which is very hard on the common USGS scale. That affects water heaters and fixtures in every city we serve.

Permit rules are not the same everywhere. Each city sets its own process, so we check with the building department in your city before starting permitted work. In Cypress, the Building Division lists water heaters and plumbing work among the work that needs a permit.

How we choose who to send

Dispatch looks at the type of call, the equipment needed and the distance. A sewer backup needs a heavy machine and a camera. A burst pipe needs fittings and a cutter. A water heater failure needs a unit that fits the space.

We keep the main tools on each truck, so the first visit usually solves the problem or confirms exactly what is needed. When a part or a unit has to be ordered, we tell you before leaving.

Hours, holidays and storms

The line is open 24 hours a day, every day. Holidays and weekends are busy for burst pipes, overflowing toilets and failed water heaters, and the dispatcher handles those first.

During heavy rain, storm drains and area drains can back up. If water is rising around a building, tell the dispatcher so the right crew is assigned.

Common calls by city

In Cypress and Los Alamitos, we see many failing water heaters and pinhole leaks in older copper. In La Palma, drain backups and aging lines are frequent. In Stanton, large households and shared buildings put steady demand on water heaters and drains. In Buena Park, restaurants, apartment communities and homes all call.

Whichever city you are in, the first step is the same. Close the main valve if water is moving, and call the 24/7 line.
