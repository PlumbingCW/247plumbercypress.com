What the Cypress Business Park is

The City of Cypress housing plan describes the Cypress Business Park as concentrated along Katella Avenue, west of Knott Avenue, covering about 800 acres. It is an employment district of offices, warehouses and light industrial space. It is not a residential subdivision, and this page is not a neighborhood page for homes.

We found no single published parcel map of the park, so we ask for the building address and the cross street. Katella Avenue is the anchor, and Knott Avenue is the eastern marker.

How commercial plumbing calls differ

Offices and warehouses depend on restrooms, water heaters, floor drains and fire or process lines that residential homes seldom have. A restroom outage can send staff home. A blocked floor drain can leave standing water on a warehouse floor.

That is general commercial plumbing information and not a claim about any one building. Our commercial plumbing page explains the work in more detail.

Tenant or landlord

In a leased unit, your lease usually decides who handles plumbing inside the space and who handles the building lines. Read it before you hire anyone, and tell your property manager as soon as water is moving.

A plumber can stop water and diagnose the problem at once. Who pays is a separate conversation, and a written finding and photographs make that conversation shorter.

Water, backflow and permits

Golden State Water supplies Cypress, and its report shows average hardness of 237 mg/L, which affects commercial water heaters too. Some commercial buildings carry backflow assemblies, and those may need periodic testing by a certified tester. Ask your water provider whether your building is on a testing schedule.

Plumbing work and water heater replacement in Cypress need a City of Cypress building permit. The Building Division at (714) 229-6730 can confirm the steps.

Standards and checking a license

For commercial work, confirm the contractor's license with the Contractors State License Board at cslb.ca.gov or 1-800-321-CSLB (2752). Plumbing in California follows the California Plumbing Code, and your property manager may require proof of license and insurance before anyone works in the building.
