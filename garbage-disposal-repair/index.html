Safety first with a stuck disposal

Never put a hand into a disposal, even when it is off. Switch off the wall switch and the breaker, then look from above with a flashlight. Many jams can be cleared by turning the motor shaft by hand with a hex key from underneath, but a technician can do that safely and check the cause.

A humming sound means the motor is trying to spin and cannot. Silence usually means a tripped overload button or no power at all.

Common disposal faults

Hums but does not spin

A jam from bones, fruit pits or a utensil. Clearing the jam usually restores it.

No sound

Reset button tripped or an outlet issue. A short check finds out.

Leaks from the bottom

Housing seal failure. This usually means replacement.

Leaks at the sink flange or drain

Loose mounting or a worn gasket, a repair can fix it.

Repair or replace

Disposals generally last about eight to twelve years. If the unit is leaking from the housing, replacement is the only fix. If it is a jam, a tripped reset or a loose connection, repair is cheap and quick.

A disposal that keeps clogging may be pointing to a drain line problem rather than a disposal problem. Kitchen drains collect grease over time, and a cable or jetting visit may be the real fix, see drain cleaning.

What not to put in a disposal

  • Fats, oils and grease, which coat the drain line.
  • Bones, fruit pits and shells.
  • Coffee grounds in large amounts.
  • Stringy vegetables such as celery and corn husks.
  • Potato peels and rice in quantity, which turn to paste.

Installing a new disposal

A replacement disposal is sized for how you use the kitchen. A smaller unit suits a single person, and a larger motor suits a household that cooks daily. We remove the old unit, clean the sink flange, mount the new one, reconnect the drain and the dishwasher inlet and test for leaks.

We also check the electrical connection. A disposal needs a dedicated outlet or hardwired connection with a switch, and an old kitchen may need that updated.

Why the kitchen drain matters

A disposal is only as good as the drain behind it. Grease that passes through the disposal still coats the pipe downstream. If the sink backs up even with a working disposal, the line needs cleaning, and our technicians can do it on the same visit.

Standards and checking a plumber's license

Plumbing work for garbage disposal repair in California is governed by the California Plumbing Code, and plumbing contractors must hold a license from the Contractors State License Board.

You can look up any contractor on the CSLB License Check tool at cslb.ca.gov, or call 1-800-321-CSLB (2752). Ask for our license details when you call and check them yourself before work begins.

Safety steps before touching a disposal

  • Switch off the wall switch and the breaker.
  • Never put a hand or tool into the chamber, even when it is off.
  • Unplug the unit if it is plug in.
  • Use a hex key at the bottom of the unit to free the motor shaft.
  • Press the red reset button only after the jam is cleared.
