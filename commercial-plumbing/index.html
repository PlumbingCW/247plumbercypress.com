Plumbing problems that stop a business

A restaurant that cannot drain its prep sinks cannot serve food. A medical office without hot water may have to cancel patients. A retail center with sewage in a restroom closes it. Commercial plumbing emergencies are about keeping the doors open, and the first call should describe what is closed.

We prioritize restoring the minimum working service, then schedule the permanent repair at a time that suits your hours.

What we handle

Drains and grease lines

Heavy use and cooking fat clog commercial lines faster than home ones. See hydro jetting and sewer line cleaning.

Water heaters

Larger tanks and tankless banks serve kitchens and restrooms. Failures are handled under water heater replacement.

Restrooms

Flush valves, toilets, urinals and sinks in public use take more abuse than home fixtures.

Supply lines

Leaks and bursts in larger diameter lines are repaired or replaced, see burst pipe repair.

Grease traps, backflow and storm drains

Kitchens use grease traps or interceptors to catch fats before they reach the sewer. They need regular cleaning, and a full one backs up the kitchen. Backflow preventers keep dirty water from returning to the potable supply, and many need testing by a certified tester on a schedule set by the local water provider.

Storm drains and area drains in parking lots and loading areas clog with leaves and trash. A blocked one floods a parking lot after rain. Tell us which of these apply to your site so the right crew arrives.

Working around your business

We ask the questions that matter to an operator: when do you open, which fixtures can be out of service, who has keys, and who approves the work. Quiet hours let us do noisy or disruptive tasks without losing customers.

For property managers, we keep a record of the work done on each building so the next visit starts with what the last one found. That history saves time on a problem that returns.

Standards and checking a plumber's license

Plumbing work for commercial plumbing in California is governed by the California Plumbing Code, and plumbing contractors must hold a license from the Contractors State License Board.

You can look up any contractor on the CSLB License Check tool at cslb.ca.gov, or call 1-800-321-CSLB (2752). Ask for our license details when you call and check them yourself before work begins.
