A small city with a plumbing list to match its age

La Palma was incorporated on October 26, 1955 under the name Dairyland, and took its present name in 1965 after the dairies moved out. It covers 1.8 square miles with about 15,600 residents and roughly 5,300 housing units, about two thirds of them owner occupied.

Owner occupied homes mean owners decide when to repair and when to replace. Many of those houses went up in the late 1950s and 1960s, and the supply and drain lines that came with them are now at an age where planned work is cheaper than emergencies.

The regional sewer line and what it means for you

La Palma is served by the Cypress Trunk Sewer, a roughly fifty year old regional line of about seven miles that the Orange County Sanitation District is rehabilitating. The work plans to replace around a mile of pipe, clean and grout about five and a half miles and rehabilitate or replace roughly a hundred manholes.

That project strengthens the main sewer in the street. It does not touch the lateral that runs from your house, which is your responsibility. A camera check of that lateral is the best way to know where yours stands, see sewer inspection.

Planning ahead for an older La Palma home

With 68 percent of homes owner occupied, La Palma residents usually decide for themselves when to invest in a system. A tank that is eight to twelve years old, a drain that clogs twice a year or a copper line with its first pinhole are all prompts to plan the work in daylight, with a clear price, instead of at midnight.

A short visit can check shutoff valves, water pressure, the water heater and the cleanout. The findings help you decide what to do now, what to watch and what can wait.

A homeowner's five minute plumbing check

  • Find and test the main shutoff valve.
  • Look under every sink for slow drips or stains.
  • Check the water heater base for rust or moisture.
  • Locate the sewer cleanout and make sure it can be reached.
  • Read the water meter with everything off and again an hour later.

Checking a plumber's license in La Palma

Any plumbing contractor working in La Palma should be licensed by the California Contractors State License Board, and work should follow the California Plumbing Code.

The CSLB License Check tool at cslb.ca.gov lets you verify a license, and the board answers questions at 1-800-321-CSLB (2752).
