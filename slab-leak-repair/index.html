What a slab leak is and why it matters

Most homes in Cypress sit on a concrete slab foundation, and water lines can run through or under it. A leak in one of those lines saturates the soil under the slab, can lift flooring and can erode support over time. The hot water side is easier to notice because the floor warms above it.

Because the pipe is buried, finding the leak precisely is the first and most important step. A wrong guess means cutting concrete in the wrong place.

What to do the moment you suspect a slab leak

Close the main

Turn off the main valve and check whether the meter stops. If it does, the leak is on the house side.

Shut off the water heater

If the leak is on the hot side, turn off the heater so the tank does not draw from a compromised line.

Lift valuables

Move rugs and furniture and photograph damage for your insurer.

Mark the hot spots

Note where the floor feels warm or damp so the technician can start there.

How we locate the break

We confirm the leak with a pressure test, then pass thermal imaging and acoustic equipment over the floor to find the point where water escapes. The result is marked on the floor before anything is cut. A small, accurate opening keeps repair cost and mess down.

Why slab leaks happen in this area

Copper lines installed in the 1960s through the 1980s can develop pinhole leaks from the inside. Soil movement, pipes rubbing against concrete and high water pressure all add stress. Readings in its 2025 data ranged from roughly 63 to 369 mg/L.

The local supply is hard, and Golden State Water reports an average hardness of 237 mg/L, or about 13.8 grains per gallon, for its West Orange County system. Mineral content and water chemistry both affect how fast copper corrodes.

Cypress homes from the post dairy farm building boom often have original copper or even galvanized lines that are well past their design life. A slab leak in one is a clue about the rest, which is why we discuss options openly rather than just patching.

Standards and checking a plumber's license

Plumbing work for slab leak repair in California is governed by the California Plumbing Code, and plumbing contractors must hold a license from the Contractors State License Board.

You can look up any contractor on the CSLB License Check tool at cslb.ca.gov, or call 1-800-321-CSLB (2752). Ask for our license details when you call and check them yourself before work begins.
