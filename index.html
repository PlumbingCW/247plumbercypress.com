How emergency plumbing works in Cypress

When water is moving where it should not, minutes matter. The first call should go to a person who can tell you how to stop the water and who can send a technician with the right tools. Our line is answered 24/7, and the dispatcher asks four things: what you see, where it is, how old the house is and whether the water is still running.

Those answers decide who we send and what goes in the truck. A failed water heater needs a replacement unit or parts. A sewer backup needs a heavy machine and a camera. A burst pipe needs a shutoff, a cutter and fittings.

Homes in Cypress and the plumbing they carry

Cypress was incorporated on July 24, 1956 as Dairy City, and residents voted to rename it Cypress on August 6, 1957, after Cypress Elementary School. In the 1960s the dairies were sold to developers, Cypress College opened in 1966 and the Civic Center in 1967. Much of the housing dates from that building boom.

For a homeowner, that history translates into aging galvanized and early copper supply lines, original water heaters near the end of their life and sewer laterals that have seen decades of roots. Knowing the age of your house tells us what to expect, which is why we ask about it on the first call.

Water, permits and the regional sewer

Cypress is served by Golden State Water Company. Its 2026 water quality report, covering 2025 data, lists average hardness in the West Orange County system near 237 mg/L, or 13.8 grains per gallon. That is hard water, and it shortens the life of water heaters and fixtures. Our water softener and filtration service addresses it.

The City of Cypress Building Division requires permits for water heaters and plumbing work, with inspections Monday through Friday from 8:00 a.m. to 4:00 p.m. The regional Cypress Trunk Sewer, a line about fifty years old that serves Cypress, Los Alamitos and La Palma, is being rehabilitated by the Orange County Sanitation District. Your own lateral is separate, and a camera inspection shows its condition.

What to do in the first ten minutes

Close the main water valve, usually near the meter or in the garage. Open the lowest faucet to drain the pressure. Switch off breakers to wet areas if you can do it from a dry spot. Stop running drains if a line is blocked. Photograph everything and call the 24/7 line.

If you smell gas, leave the home and call the gas utility and 911 from outside. A plumber cannot work safely until the gas is cleared.
