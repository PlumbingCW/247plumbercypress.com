How to Install a Switch for a Garbage Disposal: What Cypress, CA Homes Need to Know

By the Cypress 24/7 Plumbing team. Published November 12, 2026.

A garbage disposal needs a way to turn it on and off, and there are two common ways: a wall switch connected to the unit's wiring, or an air switch mounted on the countertop that sends a puff of air to a control box. Both work well. The right choice depends on the wiring that already exists in your kitchen.

This guide explains how each option works, the safety steps before any electrical work and when to call a professional instead of doing it yourself.

Wall switch or air switch

A wall switch is the standard choice. It controls a circuit that powers either a hardwired disposal or an outlet under the sink. An air switch has a button on the counter or sink rim and a small tube leading to a module that closes the circuit. It needs no electricity at the button, which some people prefer near water.

If a kitchen has an outlet under the sink but no switch, an air switch is often the cleaner retrofit. If the cabinet has a switched outlet, the switch already exists somewhere on the wall.

Safety before any wiring work

Turn off the breaker and test the wires with a non contact voltage tester before touching anything. If the disposal is hardwired and you are not comfortable with electrical work, hire a licensed electrician. A mistake with wiring near water can cause shock or fire.

The basic steps for an air switch

Drill a hole in the countertop or use an existing sink hole, install the button and run the tube to the control module under the sink. Plug the module into an outlet and the disposal into the module. Test it with the water running.

Check that the tube is not kinked and that the module is mounted above any possible water. After installation, run the disposal for a few seconds to confirm it starts and stops cleanly.

Permits and local rules

Plumbing work and water heaters need permits in the City of Cypress, according to its Building Division. Electrical work may also need a permit depending on scope, and replacing a simple switch is different from running a new circuit. Call the Building Division at (714) 229-6730 if you are unsure.

We install disposals and can coordinate the electrical piece. Customers in Buena Park and Los Alamitos should check with their own building departments, because rules are set locally.

If the disposal itself is the problem

A disposal that will not start may have a tripped reset button on the bottom, a jammed flywheel or a failed motor, not a bad switch. Check those before replacing the switch. If the unit hums and does not turn, turn it off and see the garbage disposal repair page for the steps.

A disposal that leaks, or one that is more than a decade old, is a candidate for replacement, which is a good moment to add a new switch.

Reasons switches fail

Wall switches wear out after years of use, and the contacts can burn or loosen. Air switch tubes crack and develop leaks that stop the pulse reaching the module. Wiring in older kitchens can degrade near the cabinet where it is exposed to moisture.

A switch that sparks, feels hot or works intermittently should be replaced promptly by someone qualified, because the cause may be a loose connection.

Questions to ask the installer

  • Is the disposal hardwired or plug in?
  • Where will the switch be mounted?
  • Does the circuit have enough capacity for the unit?
  • Is a permit needed for the electrical part?
  • Will they test the unit before they leave?

Where to mount the control

Mount a wall switch within easy reach and away from the sink, and mount an air switch button where children cannot press it casually. Label the circuit at the panel so anyone working on the kitchen knows which breaker controls it.

Testing the finished work

Run cold water, switch the disposal on for a few seconds and listen. It should hum smoothly and stop when you press the switch. Check under the sink for leaks and for any warm connections. If something smells like burning or the breaker trips, switch it off and call a professional.

What to gather before you start

  • The model number of the disposal.
  • A non contact voltage tester.
  • The air switch kit or a wall switch and cable.
  • A drill with a bit for the countertop hole.
  • A bucket and towels for the cabinet.
