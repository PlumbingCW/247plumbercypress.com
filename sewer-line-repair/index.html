Signs the sewer line has failed

A sewer lateral is the pipe that carries wastewater from the house to the connection with the main in the street. When it fails, the signs are consistent: multiple fixtures draining slowly, gurgling sounds, sewage smells in the yard, wet or sunken ground over the line, and backups that return soon after a cleaning.

Not all of these are emergencies on their own, but a backup into the house always is. Sewage carries bacteria, so avoid contact and keep children and pets away until it is cleaned.

What Cypress homeowners should know about the lateral and the main

The sewer lateral and the public sewer in the street are separate pieces. The Orange County Sanitation District is rehabilitating the regional trunk sewer that serves Cypress, Los Alamitos and La Palma.

That roughly seven mile line is about fifty years old, and the project plans to replace around a mile of it, clean and grout about five and a half miles and rehabilitate or replace around a hundred manholes.

That work does not repair the pipe between your house and the street. If you are unsure where your responsibility ends, check with the City of Cypress or your city's public works office before an excavation, because ownership varies by property and city.

Repair options

A spot repair replaces one failed section and is the least invasive choice when the rest of the pipe is sound. Full replacement is better when the line has multiple failures. Trenchless methods such as lining can avoid digging up a driveway or patio, but they need a clear, clean pipe and are not suitable for every failure.

We explain the options with the camera footage open in front of you, so the recommendation comes from what is in the pipe and not from a script.

Standards and checking a plumber's license

Plumbing work for sewer line repair in California is governed by the California Plumbing Code, and plumbing contractors must hold a license from the Contractors State License Board.

You can look up any contractor on the CSLB License Check tool at cslb.ca.gov, or call 1-800-321-CSLB (2752). Ask for our license details when you call and check them yourself before work begins.
