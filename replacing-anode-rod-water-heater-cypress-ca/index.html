Replacing the Anode Rod on a Water Heater: A Cypress, CA Homeowner Guide

By the Cypress 24/7 Plumbing team. Published November 12, 2026.

The anode rod is a metal rod inside your water heater that corrodes so the steel tank does not. When it is used up, the tank starts to rust. Checking the rod every two to three years, and replacing it when it is mostly bare steel, is the cheapest way to extend a water heater's life.

This guide explains how the rod works, how to tell when it needs replacing, what the job involves and how Cypress water affects the schedule.

Why the rod exists

Tank water heaters have a steel shell lined with glass. Tiny cracks in the glass are normal, and water reaches the steel through them. A sacrificial rod made of magnesium or aluminum is attached to the tank, and because those metals corrode more easily than steel, they take the damage first.

When the rod has corroded down to a thin wire, the steel is next. A heater without a working rod can rust through years earlier than it should.

Signs the rod is worn

You might notice rust colored or smelly hot water, a rumbling tank, or an anode rod that looks pencil thin when inspected. Some rods are visible with the cold water supply closed and the plug on top removed. Rotten egg smells are sometimes caused by bacteria reacting with magnesium rods, and a technician can suggest an alternative metal.

The best sign is the age of the tank. If the heater is five years old and the rod has never been inspected, it is time to look.

How the replacement is done

The technician turns off the power or gas, closes the cold water inlet and relieves pressure from the tank. A short length of water is drained, the hex head on the rod is loosened with a long socket or breaker bar and the old rod is pulled out. Many rods are stuck, so a good tool and room above the heater matter.

A new rod is installed with thread sealant, the tank is refilled and checked for leaks and the power is restored. The old rod tells the story. If it is half gone, the new one should last several years.

When replacement is no longer worthwhile

If the tank has already rusted at the base, leaks from the shell or is more than about twelve years old, a new anode rod will not rescue it. See water heater replacement for what to do in that case, and call the 24/7 line if water is on the floor.

Hard water in Cypress means a faster schedule

Cypress is served by Golden State Water Company. Golden State Water reports an average hardness of 237 mg/L, or about 13.8 grains per gallon, for its West Orange County system. Readings in its 2025 data ranged from roughly 63 to 369 mg/L.

Hard water does not directly eat the rod, but the warm water and dissolved minerals promote a fast electrochemical reaction, and the rod is used up sooner than the manufacturer's average suggests.

The supply blends local groundwater with imported water, so conditions shift. The practical advice is to check the rod every two years in this area, drain the tank annually and consider a water softener. Neighbors in Stanton and Los Alamitos are served by the same utility and see similar wear.

A simple yearly water heater routine

  • Drain a few gallons from the tank drain valve to check for sediment and rust.
  • Look at the base of the tank for moisture and rust.
  • Test the temperature and pressure relief valve by lifting the lever briefly, with a bucket below.
  • Check the shutoff valves for leaks or stiffness.
  • Inspect the anode rod every two to three years.

A realistic view of what the rod can and cannot do

An anode rod slows corrosion of the tank. It cannot repair rust that has already formed, and it does not prevent leaks from fittings. It also cannot stop mineral scale, which is a separate problem solved by flushing and softening.

If you inspect a rod and find it intact, that is a good sign and you can wait another couple of years. If it is gone, replace it and plan to check it sooner next time.

What to do if you rent or lack access

Many Cypress garages have the heater in a tight closet with a low ceiling, which makes pulling a rod hard. Flexible segmented rods exist for that reason. If you rent, ask the owner to authorize the work, because the repair protects their equipment. A water heater repair visit can include a rod inspection with a flush, which is often the best use of one trip.
