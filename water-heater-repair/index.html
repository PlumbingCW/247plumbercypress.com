When no hot water is an emergency

A household without hot water for a day is a hardship, and a heater that is actively leaking is a property risk. Callers in Cypress describe a short list of symptoms: lukewarm water, a pilot flame that keeps going out, a clicking igniter, a tripped breaker, rust colored hot water and water on the garage floor.

Each symptom points to a different part. We ask which one you are seeing because it decides what the technician brings.

Symptoms and likely causes

Pilot will not stay lit

A worn thermocouple or a dirty pilot assembly is the usual cause on gas tanks. The part is inexpensive and the fix is quick, which is why people search for thermocouple replacement.

Water is lukewarm

A failed lower heating element on an electric tank, or a gas burner blocked by sediment, cuts the supply of hot water.

Rusty or smelly hot water

A spent anode rod lets the tank wall corrode. Replacing the rod early can add years, replacing it late cannot save the tank.

Water at the base

Check the drain valve and fittings first. A leak from the tank wall means replacement, see water heater replacement.

Why hard water shortens heater life here

Golden State Water reports an average hardness of 237 mg/L, or about 13.8 grains per gallon, for its West Orange County system. Readings in its 2025 data ranged from roughly 63 to 369 mg/L.

Scale builds on the heating surfaces, so electric elements burn out sooner and gas tanks run noisy as sediment traps steam bubbles. A yearly drain and flush helps, and a softener reduces the load at the source.

How the diagnosis works

A technician confirms the fuel type and age, checks the shutoff valves, tests the thermostat or gas control, inspects the relief valve and drain line and looks for scale or rust. For gas units, the flue and combustion air are looked at as well. The price is explained before parts are ordered.

If the repair would cost more than half the price of a new unit on an older tank, we say so plainly. A repair is only worth it if the tank still has years left.

Standards and checking a plumber's license

Plumbing work for water heater repair in California is governed by the California Plumbing Code, and plumbing contractors must hold a license from the Contractors State License Board.

You can look up any contractor on the CSLB License Check tool at cslb.ca.gov, or call 1-800-321-CSLB (2752). Ask for our license details when you call and check them yourself before work begins.
