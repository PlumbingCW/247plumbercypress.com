Whole House Repiping in Cypress, CA

Repiping replaces the water supply lines in a home when old pipes keep failing. It is a planned project, not an emergency, and the best results come from comparing options before the first leak turns into a flood.

Repiping in Cypress, CA is common in homes with galvanized steel or aging copper that shows pinholes, rusty water or falling pressure.

Signs your home is ready for a repipe

One leak does not call for a repipe. A pattern does. Repeated pinholes, brown water that clears slowly, weak flow at several fixtures and visible green corrosion at joints suggest the pipe itself is failing.

Many Cypress homes were built when galvanized steel and early copper were standard. Over decades galvanized pipe rusts inside until the opening is a fraction of its original size, and copper can develop pinholes.

Choosing the pipe

PEX

Flexible, quick to install and resistant to scale and pinholes. It cannot be used outdoors in sunlight and needs protection where it passes through framing.

Copper

Durable and familiar, with a long record. It costs more in material and labor.

CPVC

A rigid plastic used in some regions. Requirements and suitability vary, so ask which is allowed.

Hybrid

Some jobs use copper at exposed points and PEX in walls.

How a repipe works and what to expect

A crew shuts off the water, runs new lines through walls, attic or crawlspace and connects each fixture. Small access holes are cut in drywall and patched afterward. Most homes are without water for part of each day over a couple of days.

Because the work affects every room, we walk the home with you first and mark where lines will run. Houses on slabs often reroute lines overhead rather than cutting concrete, see slab leak repair.

Permits and water quality

The City of Cypress Building Division lists plumbing work among the work needing a permit. Inspections run Monday through Friday from 8:00 a.m. to 4:00 p.m., and about two days of notice is suggested. We manage the permit and schedule inspections.

Golden State Water reports an average hardness of 237 mg/L, or about 13.8 grains per gallon, for its West Orange County system. Readings in its 2025 data ranged from roughly 63 to 369 mg/L. Hard water is rough on copper and on fixtures, so a softener pairs well with a new system.

Preparing the house for a repipe

Before the crew arrives, clear access to the water heater, the main shutoff and the rooms with fixtures. Move furniture away from walls and cover valuables. Plan for short periods without water, and keep a few jugs on hand.

Tell your household about the schedule, and let any pets know where to stay. A well prepared house shortens the project and reduces the mess.

Questions to ask any repipe contractor

  • Which pipe material will be used, and why?
  • How many access holes will be cut, and who patches them?
  • Is the permit included, and who schedules the inspection?
  • How will the water be restored each evening?
  • What is the plan if the walls reveal other problems?

Standards and checking a plumber's license

Plumbing work for repiping in California is governed by the California Plumbing Code, and plumbing contractors must hold a license from the Contractors State License Board.

You can look up any contractor on the CSLB License Check tool at cslb.ca.gov, or call 1-800-321-CSLB (2752). Ask for our license details when you call and check them yourself before work begins.
