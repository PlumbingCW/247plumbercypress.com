Stanton homes and the plumbing they put to work

Stanton was incorporated on June 4, 1956. Today it has about 38,000 residents in roughly three square miles, and 11,361 housing units that are almost all occupied. Households average more than three people, and roughly half are renter occupied.

Larger households and heavy water use shape the plumbing calls. More showers, more laundry and more cooking all put extra demand on water heaters and drain lines. Renters and landlords both call us, and we explain the findings to whichever of them is on the line.

Landlords, tenants and who calls who

Because about half of Stanton's housing is rented, many calls come from tenants who are not sure whom to contact. The short answer is to call the owner or manager for permission and to call us for anything urgent. We keep the process simple and record what we found so the owner can see it.

If a pipe is flooding a unit, close the shutoff and call us first. A landlord can authorize repairs after the water has stopped. Photograph everything, because it helps with any insurance or lease discussion that follows.

Water quality, permits and local rules

Golden State Water Company serves Stanton, and Golden State Water reports an average hardness of 237 mg/L, or about 13.8 grains per gallon, for its West Orange County system. Readings in its 2025 data ranged from roughly 63 to 369 mg/L.

Hard water leaves scale on fixtures and shortens heater life. For a water heater replacement, check with the City of Stanton for its current permit process, and we will assemble the information needed.

Keeping water heaters up with a busy household

In a home with several people, the water heater works hard. A tank that was sized for a smaller family will run out during back to back showers. If your hot water fades mid shower, the cause may be a failing element, a sediment filled tank or a unit that is simply too small.

We look at the number of people, the fixtures and the space, and then recommend a repair, a larger tank or a tankless unit. Our tankless page covers how that choice works.

Ways to reduce drain problems in a full household

  • Scrape plates into the trash before washing.
  • Use hair catchers in showers and tubs.
  • Run hot water for a minute after washing greasy dishes.
  • Flush only toilet paper.
  • Schedule a cleaning if a drain slows more than once a month.

Checking a plumber's license in Stanton

Any plumbing contractor working in Stanton should be licensed by the California Contractors State License Board, and work should follow the California Plumbing Code.

The CSLB License Check tool at cslb.ca.gov lets you verify a license, and the board answers questions at 1-800-321-CSLB (2752).
