Should I Repipe My House in Cypress, CA?

By the Cypress 24/7 Plumbing team. Published November 12, 2026.

You should consider repiping when leaks are no longer isolated, when the water runs rusty or when pressure keeps falling even though the main valve is open. One leak in a sound line is a repair. A pattern of leaks in an aging line is a repipe.

This guide gives you a plain way to decide, with the signs to watch for, the alternatives to compare and the questions that separate a sound recommendation from a sales pitch.

Signs the pipes are done

Look for pinhole leaks in copper, green or white crust at joints, rusty water that comes and goes, low pressure on more than one fixture and repeated repairs in the same line. A burst once in ten years is bad luck. Three leaks in two years is a trend.

Pipe material also matters. Galvanized steel from before the 1960s corrodes from the inside out. Copper installed in the following decades can pit under certain water conditions.

Questions to ask yourself

How old is the house, and what pipe does it have? How many leaks have you had, and where? Are you planning to remodel soon, which would open the walls anyway? Do you plan to stay long enough to benefit? A repipe is a long term investment and is worth most to owners who plan to stay.

If you are selling, a repipe can remove an objection. If you are staying, it removes the risk of repeated water damage.

The age of Cypress housing

Cypress was incorporated as Dairy City in 1956 and renamed in 1957. The dairies sold to developers in the 1960s, and Cypress College opened in 1966. A large share of the city's housing comes from that period, which is exactly when galvanized and early copper lines were the norm. Homes in La Palma and Los Alamitos share that history.

Local water adds to the picture. Golden State Water reports an average hardness of 237 mg/L, or about 13.8 grains per gallon, for its West Orange County system. Readings in its 2025 data ranged from roughly 63 to 369 mg/L.

Hard water is rough on copper and leaves scale that narrows pipes. Permits for plumbing work are issued through the City of Cypress Building Division, and we handle them.

Alternatives to consider

A partial repipe replaces the worst branch while leaving the rest. A reroute abandons a leaking slab line and runs a new one overhead. A water softener slows scale in lines that are otherwise sound. Pressure regulation reduces stress on every fitting. See leak detection if you are not sure how many leaks there are.

The right answer is the one the evidence supports. A plumber should show you the failed pipe, explain why and then offer more than one option.

A simple scoring approach

Give one point for each of the following: pipe older than fifty years, two or more leaks in two years, rusty water, low pressure on several fixtures, galvanized material and a plan to stay more than ten years. Three or more points suggests getting a repipe quote. Fewer than three suggests monitoring the system and fixing leaks as they appear.

The score is a rough guide, not a verdict. A plumber who inspects your lines can refine it with real evidence.

What life looks like after a repipe

Owners often report steady pressure, clear water and fewer worries about hidden leaks. The new system comes with new shutoff valves at each fixture, which makes later repairs simpler. Some contractors also add a pressure regulator and an expansion tank when the existing ones are worn.

A repipe is not a cure for every plumbing issue. Drains, water heaters and fixtures are separate systems. Ask what the project covers and what it leaves alone.

Documents to keep after the work

  • The permit and final inspection record.
  • The pipe material and manufacturer information.
  • A diagram or photos of where lines run.
  • The contractor warranty in writing.
  • Photos taken before the walls were closed.

Next steps if you are leaning toward yes

Ask a plumber for a written inspection of what you have, including pipe material, the number of leaks and the pressure reading. Get at least two scoped quotes. Decide on timing. If you are comfortable, schedule the work before the next leak does it for you.

What a good recommendation looks like

It names the pipe material, shows you the damage, lists the options with their tradeoffs and gives a price in writing. If someone pushes you to sign today, walk away. For the process and what to expect, read the repiping page.
