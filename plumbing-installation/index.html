What we install

Kitchens and bathrooms are the busiest jobs. New sinks, faucets, toilets, showers, tubs and dishwasher supply lines all need correct drains, vents and shutoffs. Laundry rooms, outdoor bibs and gas lines for appliances are common add ons.

Each fixture ties into the water supply and the drain system. A good installation respects both, and the vent that lets the drain run properly is often the part DIY work misses.

Permits, inspections and Cypress homes

The City of Cypress Building Division requires permits for plumbing work, and inspections run Monday through Friday from 8:00 a.m. to 4:00 p.m. A permit and a passed inspection protect you when you sell, and they confirm that the new work meets current code.

Cypress has many homes that date to the 1960s and 1970s. Tying new lines into old galvanized or early copper can create weak points, so we check the connection and recommend upgrades where needed.

Drains, vents and slope: the unseen part

Every drain needs a steady slope, usually a quarter inch per foot for small lines, so water and waste move along without leaving solids behind. Every fixture needs a vent so air can enter behind the draining water. Without both, a drain gurgles, drains slowly or lets sewer gas into the room.

On a remodel, this is the part most worth getting right. Tile, cabinets and paint cover the pipes quickly, and fixing a drain slope afterward means reopening the work.

Shutoff valves and future repairs

We put a shutoff valve on every new fixture so that the next repair does not require closing the water to the whole house. It is a small cost during installation and a large convenience years later.

We label the main lines in the garage or utility area if you ask, and we show you the location of every valve we touched. A homeowner who knows where the shutoffs are can limit the damage of any future leak.

What to have ready before installation day

  • Fixtures and fittings on site, with the model numbers written down.
  • Clear access to sinks, walls and the water heater area.
  • A decision on the finish materials so rough in dimensions fit.
  • A plan for where the water will be off and for how long.
  • Any permit documents from your contractor, if they are handling them.

Standards and checking a plumber's license

Plumbing work for plumbing installation in California is governed by the California Plumbing Code, and plumbing contractors must hold a license from the Contractors State License Board.

You can look up any contractor on the CSLB License Check tool at cslb.ca.gov, or call 1-800-321-CSLB (2752). Ask for our license details when you call and check them yourself before work begins.
