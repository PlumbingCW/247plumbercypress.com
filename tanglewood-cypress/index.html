About Tanglewood

Local listings refer to the community as Tanglewood, and some use Tanglewood North for one section of it. One real estate profile says it was built in 1968 and reports 443 attached townhomes, with seven pools at four locations and two clubhouses. We could not confirm those figures with an official source, so treat them as reported.

Sources place Tanglewood on the west side of Cypress, and one competitor page puts it near Oak Knoll Park at 9600 Graham Street. No published boundary separates Tanglewood from Tanglewood North, so give us your street and cross street when you call.

What attached homes change

Townhomes share walls with their neighbors. In general that means a pipe inside a shared wall may serve more than one home, and a leak can reach a neighbor before you notice it. That is general plumbing information and not a claim about any particular building in Tanglewood.

If a leak may have crossed a wall, tell your association manager and your neighbor early. Photograph the damage and the source before anything is moved.

Reading your association documents

One local profile says the association maintains lawns, trees and bushes, and that it has an on site manager. It does not say which plumbing lines the association handles. Your governing documents decide that, so read them before you commission work on a line that may be shared.

A plumber can stop water and diagnose the problem at any point. The question of who pays is separate, and having a photo record and a written finding makes that conversation easier.

Hard water and permits

Tap water in Cypress comes from Golden State Water, whose West Orange County system averaged 237 mg/L of hardness in 2025 data. Scale shortens water heater life, and a softener or filter can slow it.

Water heater replacement needs a City of Cypress building permit. The Building Division at (714) 229-6730 can confirm steps. Associations often add their own rules for work that affects shared elements.

Standards and checking a license

Whoever you hire, verify the contractor's license first. Work in California follows the California Plumbing Code, and the Contractors State License Board lets you look up a license at cslb.ca.gov or by phone at 1-800-321-CSLB (2752). Association rules may ask for proof before work starts.
